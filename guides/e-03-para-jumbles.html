1. Snapshot

Weight: about 2 per shift (43 jumbles in the 22-shift file; 4-part is dominant, 5-part is rare). Marks: 1 each, −¼ wrong. 🔥
How it is asked: (a) 4 segments P-Q-R-S, no anchor; (b) anchor statement, then arrange P-Q-R-S; (c) first segment fixed; (d) A-B-C-D / A-E segments forming ONE sentence. Topics: academic, policy, science, law, economy. Options are four orderings, usually differing only in the first and last positions.
  1. Read all segments once (10 s).
  2. Find the segment that can start a sentence: has a subject, no dangling "who/which/that/to/by/in".
  3. Anchor given? Find the segment that grammatically continues it.
  4. Look for rigid pairs (not only…but also, no sooner…than, unless…).
  5. Join subject → verb → object.
  6. Then add modifiers: purpose (to…), place/scope (in/across), time (during/before).
  7. Participles (-ing) and relative clauses attach to the noun just before.
  8. Eliminate options by first segment, then by last segment.
  9. Read the full sentence aloud in your head.
  10. Never spend over 45 s; guess after eliminating 2.

3. Method and 10 worked examples

Opening sentence: a general statement, a subject noun phrase, or an opener word (Although, Having, Notwithstanding). Never starts with a pronoun lacking antecedent, with "to/by/in/that/who", or with a participle.
Mandatory pairs: pronoun and noun; however/but/therefore link to the previous idea; this/that point back; not only…but also; no sooner…than; unless…will.
Flow: cause then effect; problem then solution; general then specific.
Conclusion check: the last piece is usually a scope/place/time tail, a participle or a result.
Eliminate: once one fixed pair is known, strike options that break it.
30-second drill: 0-5 s read all; 5-12 s pick opener; 12-20 s lock one pair; 20-25 s eliminate; 25-30 s read-through and mark. If not locked at 30 s, eliminate and move on.

Common opener patterns

  • Subject + verb: "Collaborative learning environments have proven…"
  • Conjunction: "Though…", "When…", "Unless…", "Since…"
  • Prepositional: "Notwithstanding…", "Despite…", "In recent years,…"
  • Participle: "Having examined…" (then subject follows)
  • Negative inversion: "Not only did…", "Rarely have…", "Only by… can…", "Hardly had… when"
  • Impersonal: "It is imperative for… to…"

5. Memory aids

S-V-O-P-S: Subject, Verb, Object, Purpose (to…), Scope (in/across/during).
Tail rule: segments starting with to / by / in / across / during / -ing / which usually come AFTER the clause they modify.
Pairs: not only…but also · no sooner…than · hardly…when · rarely…than/as · only…can · neither…nor.
Confusable: "that" after a verb of saying starts the object clause; "that" after a noun starts a relative clause.

6. Trap alerts (5 patterns)

  1. Swapped tail: two options differ only in the order of the last two segments (purpose vs scope).
  2. False opener: a segment that looks complete but is really a modifier.
  3. Inversion hidden: "can growing firms expect" looks wrong but is correct after "only by".
  4. Anchor ignored: the first segment must grammatically follow the anchor (comma or "that").
  5. Subordinate clause placement: "Though…" can precede or follow the main clause; test both and use the leftover segments.
Skip unless time is left

Subjunctive inversion with "Were it not for…", parallel structure in long five-part chains, rare conjunctive adverbs (notwithstanding as a postposition).

8. 60-second revision card

  1. Find the opener first.
  2. Opener never starts with to/by/in/that/who/-ing.
  3. Anchor ends: next piece is a subject or "that".
  4. Subject, verb, object, purpose, scope.
  5. to/by/in/across segments trail.
  6. -ing phrase attaches to the noun before it.
  7. Which/who clause follows the noun it describes.
  8. Not only…but also: inversion after "not only".
  9. No sooner…than; hardly…when.
  10. Rarely/seldom/hardly: auxiliary before subject.
  11. Only by…: auxiliary before subject.
  12. However/but/therefore link back.
  13. Although/though/while/unless: subordinate clause.
  14. Fixed first segment cannot move.
  15. Eliminate by first then last segment.
  16. Read the whole sentence before marking.
  17. 30 s cap, then eliminate and move.
