1. Snapshot

The source file has 64 questions (~2.9 per shift). Roughly 3 in 4 are a plain definition → word (fear, study of, killing of, person who…). The rest embed the definition in a sentence (highlighted group of words → one word), plus a few synonym, idiom and grammar items filed under the same tag.

  1. Break the word: prefix / root / suffix usually gives the answer.
  2. -phobia fear · -mania obsession · -ology study · -cide killing · -cracy rule · -phile lover.
  3. For sentence type, replace the phrase with each option and check tone (praise vs blame, good vs bad).
  4. Learn pairs, not words: eulogy/panegyric, abrogation/abdication, tact/finesse.
  5. Two options that look right? Find the narrower one (eulogy = praise of the dead; panegyric = any praise).
  6. Option with a different prefix is the usual distractor (endo/exo, mono/poly, a/an).
  7. Same-family distractors (all -phobias, all -cides): match the root noun (soror = sister).
  8. Do not skip: one wrong costs ¼ mark, so eliminate to 2 before guessing.
  9. Revise the 60-second card the night before.
  10. Spend ≤30 seconds per item.

6. Trap alerts

  • Relevance trio: pertinent / germane / relevant all mean "related". Extraneous = irrelevant (opposite). Integral = essential part. The question text decides.
  • endo- vs exo-: endogamy marries inside the group, exogamy outside.
  • mono- vs poly-: one spouse vs many.
  • agora vs claustro: open/crowded vs closed spaces. Acro = height, hydro = water.
  • Praise speeches: eulogy (funeral) vs panegyric/encomium (general praise); jeremiad/tirade are blame, the reverse.
  • -cide: soror = sister, frater = brother, uxor = wife, pater = father, mater = mother, rex = king.
  • Law words: abrogation (repeal a law) vs abdication (give up power) vs abnegation (renounce belief) vs usurpation (seize power).
  • Hypocrisy vs sanctimony: sanctimony is the show of moral superiority.
  • Ambiguity: equivocal = deliberately ambiguous; ambivalent = mixed feelings; unequivocal = clear.
  • Philologist vs etymologist vs linguist vs lexicographer: texts, word origins, structure of language, dictionary-maker.
  • Amanuensis vs copyist vs secretary: takes dictation vs copies vs administers.
