1. Snapshot

Weight: ~1 Q per shift (23 Qs in 22 shifts, avg 59% correct; easy ones 71–96%, “why/formula” ones as low as 17–35%). Questions are one-liners: law/phenomenon name, effect-explanation, or a 1-step numeric.

  1. Learn the 3 laws with 1 daily example each (seat belt, recoil, braking).
  2. At the top of a throw: v = 0 but a = g downward.
  3. No air: all masses fall together; mechanical energy constant.
  4. Echo: 2d = v × t (distance is half the path).
  5. Light: TIR (fibres), refraction (bent pencil), dispersion (prism), scattering (sky).
  6. Violet: shortest λ, bends most, slowest. Red: longest λ, bends least.
  7. Convex lens F–2F → real, inverted, magnified.
  8. v = fλ; frequency up → wavelength down.
  9. Vector: displacement, velocity, force, momentum, acceleration. Scalar: speed, distance, mass, time, energy, work.
  10. Know units; V = IR; series/parallel; motor vs generator.

3. Concept notes

🔥 Inertia (1st law) – a body resists change in its state. Why seat belts: when the car stops, your body keeps moving forward; the belt applies the stopping force. Dry leaves fall when a tree is shaken (inertia of rest). Sharp turn = inertia of direction. Inertia ∝ mass.
🔥 F = ma (2nd) & 3rd law – F = rate of change of momentum. Recoil: gun and bullet get equal, opposite forces; the lighter bullet gets the huge speed (momentum conserved). Rocket works the same way.
🔥 Free fall / vertical throw – only gravity acts: a = g ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward all the time (up, top and down). At top v = 0. KE→PE going up, PE→KE coming down; total (mechanical) energy stays constant if no air. Mass does not affect fall time in vacuum.
🔥 Friction – opposes motion; why a cyclist slows after stopping pedalling. Static > sliding > rolling.
🔥 Refraction – bending due to change of speed in a new medium (frequency stays same). Bent pencil, apparent depth, lenses. Into denser medium: bends toward normal.
🔥 TIR – light going denser → rarer at angle above critical angle is fully reflected. Optical fibres (low-loss long distance), diamond sparkle.
🔥 Dispersion – each colour has a different refractive index/speed in glass, so refracts through a different angle. Violet deviates most, red least.
🔥 Scattering – short λ (blue) scatters more (Rayleigh) → blue sky; space is black (no atmosphere). Sunrise/sunset red: long path scatters blue away. Red used for danger signals (scatters least).
⭐ Sound – longitudinal, needs a medium, solid > liquid > gas. Pitch = frequency, loudness = amplitude. Echo: sound travels to wall and back, so 2d = v·t.
⭐ Electricity & magnetism – V = IR; R ∝ L/A; metals’ R rises with temperature. Motor: current + field → force (Fleming’s left). Generator: motion + field → current (Fleming’s right, induction).
🔥 Motion basics – distance (path, scalar) vs displacement (shortest, vector). Round trip: displacement 0, so average velocity 0 but average speed > 0 (Usha: 180 m in 60 s = 3 m/s, velocity 0). Average speed = total distance ÷ total time (never the mean of two speeds). Distance–time graph: slope = speed, straight line = uniform. Uniform circular motion is accelerated because direction keeps changing.
⭐ Momentum & impulse – p = mv; impulse FΔt = mΔv. Why a cricketer pulls the hand back: longer Δt, smaller force. Conservation: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ (girl 40 kg at 5 m/s onto a 3 kg cart → 4.65 m/s). Doubling momentum makes KE 4×.
⭐ Gravitation – F = Gm₁m₂/r² (Newton 1687); g = GM/R². Gravity is strongest at the surface, weaker above and below; more at poles than equator. G is universal and constant; g varies. Mass (kg, scalar, constant) vs weight (N, vector, mg).
🔥 Work, energy, power – W = Fs cosθ: positive (same direction), negative (opposite), zero (⟂, e.g. carrying a bag horizontally). Energy is the capacity to do work. Energy only changes form: dynamo/generator mech→elec, motor elec→mech, microphone sound→elec, loudspeaker elec→sound, battery chem→elec, bulb elec→light+heat, photosynthesis light→chemical. P = W/t = FV; 1 hp = 746 W.
🔥 Echo, reverberation, SONAR – echo = reflection of sound arriving after the original has faded (ear retains a sound ~0.1 s, so wall ≥ 17.2 m at ~344 m/s). Reverberation = repeated reflections in a hall. Diffraction lets you hear around corners. SONAR uses echoes underwater. Ultrasound > 20 kHz; infrasound < 20 Hz.
⭐ Mirrors & lenses (images) – concave mirror: real/inverted except between F and P (virtual, erect, enlarged). Convex mirror: always virtual, erect, diminished. Convex lens: object beyond 2F → diminished; 2F → same size; F–2F → enlarged beyond 2F; at F → infinity; inside F → virtual, enlarged. Cartesian signs: u negative; concave mirror f negative; convex mirror f positive. M = v/u (lens), −v/u (mirror).
⭐ Human eye – cornea does most refraction; iris controls pupil; retina (rods = night/B&W, cones = colour); blind spot = no receptors. Near objects: ciliary muscles contract, lens thickens. Myopia: image in front of retina → concave lens. Hypermetropia: image behind → convex lens (~after 40). Presbyopia: ageing lens (~after 50) → bifocal. Others: cataract (cloudy lens), glaucoma (optic nerve, pressure), colour blindness (cones).
⭐ Atmospheric optics – twinkling of stars, advanced sunrise/delayed sunset: refraction by air layers of changing density. Rainbow: refraction + dispersion + internal reflection in drops. Tyndall effect: scattering by colloid particles makes a beam visible.
⭐ Circuits & heating – I = q/t; R = ρL/A (alloys have higher resistivity than their metals). Series: same I, voltages add. Parallel: same V, currents add (home wiring, so one appliance off ≠ others off). H = I²Rt = VIt; P = VI. Nichrome heats, tungsten glows, fuse melts. Short circuit = live touches neutral → huge current. Earth wire is thick and carries leakage to ground.
⭐ Magnetic effect – Oersted 1820. Straight wire: concentric circles (right-hand thumb rule / Maxwell’s screw rule). Force on conductor F = BIL sinθ: maximum at 90°, zero at 0° and 180°. Solenoid: uniform field inside, behaves like a bar magnet; soft iron core makes a temporary electromagnet. Induction (Faraday 1831): relative motion of magnet and coil induces current.

5. Memory aids

  • VIBGYOR: violet → red = long λ more. Violet: short λ, high f, high energy, most bent, slowest in glass.
  • GEM = non-contact forces: Gravitational, Electrostatic, Magnetic.
  • Rich Man In Victoria Uses Xtra Gold: Radio, Micro, Infrared, Visible, UV, X-ray, Gamma (λ decreasing).
  • Convex lens = concave mirror (converging); concave lens = convex mirror (always virtual, erect, diminished).
  • Confusable pairs: mass (kg, scalar) vs weight (N, vector); speed vs velocity; distance vs displacement; pitch vs loudness; motor (left hand) vs generator (right hand); myopia (concave) vs hypermetropia (convex).
  • Discoverers: Oersted 1820 (current → magnetism); Faraday 1831 (induction); Ohm 1827; Joule heating 1840; Cavendish measured G (1798).
  • Eye mnemonic: Myopia = my far things blur → concave (minus); hyper = far-sighted → convex (plus). Presbyopia is after 50 (older than hypermetropia’s 40).
  • Lens/mirror shortcut: only real images are inverted; virtual images are always erect; convex mirror and concave lens always give virtual, erect, small images.
  • Series vs parallel: Series = Same current; Parallel = same Potential (V).
  • Fleming: left = Force (motor, “FBI”: Force, B, I); right = Generator.
  • Timeline: Newton gravitation 1687 → Cavendish G 1798 → Oersted 1820 → Ohm 1827 → Faraday 1831 → Joule 1840.

6. Trap alerts

  • Echo: using the full distance instead of 2d (the 17%-correct question).
  • “Acceleration is zero at the top” – wrong; it is g.
  • “Heavier falls faster” – only with air.
  • “Which colour bends most” → violet (not red); “fastest in glass” → red.
  • Doubling frequency halves wavelength; doubling velocity quadruples KE.
  • Lens power: convert focal length to metres first.
  • Unit pairs: Pa = N/m², W = J/s, Hz = 1/s; energy ≠ watt; kWh is energy, not power.
  • Parallel resistance is smaller than the smallest resistor.
  • Concave mirror also gives real, inverted, magnified – read whether option says lens or mirror.
  • “Mass is zero in space” (no: weight changes, mass does not); “g and G are the same” (no).
  • Round-trip problems: average velocity is zero, average speed is not.
  • Average speed of two equal-distance legs is not the arithmetic mean of the speeds.
  • Eye defect and lens pairs are often swapped: myopia concave, hypermetropia convex.
  • “Sound is faster in dry air” (no, moist is faster); “light needs a medium” (no).
  • Appliances in series (wrong) vs parallel (right); fuse in parallel (wrong).
