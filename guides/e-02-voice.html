1. Snapshot

Weight: ~2 Qs per shift (44 across 22 shifts) · priority 🔥. How it is asked: "Convert into passive/active" (≈85%), "choose the correct passive/active sentence", and the negative-inversion "correct active usage" items (Scarcely / Seldom / No sooner). Options differ only in auxiliary or participle, so the whole game is tense matching.

  1. Find the verb phrase and name its tense first.
  2. Passive = be (in the SAME tense) + V3.
  3. Old object becomes new subject; old subject goes after "by".
  4. Pronouns change: I→me, we→us, he→him, she→her, they→them (and back).
  5. Singular/plural of the new subject decides is/are, was/were, has/have.
  6. Keep the tense identical; never shift it (will have been ≠ will be).
  7. Drop vague agents (someone, people, nobody → "not").
  8. Reject options with a base verb or V-ing after "be" (e.g. "will be review").
  9. Reject options where the doer and receiver swap roles ("the report reviewed the committee").
  10. For inversion items: Scarcely/Hardly → had … when; No sooner → had … than; Seldom → does/did + V1.

5. Memory aids

  • "BE-V3" formula: only the be part carries the tense: is/are · was/were · will be · has/have been · had been · will have been.
  • Count the "been": perfect tenses always contain been; simple tenses never do.
  • "Being" = continuous (is/was being) – the only place "being" appears in an ordinary passive.
  • Scarcely–When, No sooner–Than: "SW" ("sweet when") vs "NT" ("no thanks").
  • Confusable pair: was being + V3 (action in progress) vs had been + V3 (completed earlier).
  • Confusable pair: will be + V3 vs will have been + V3 (the "by tomorrow/before" clue → future perfect).

6. Trap alerts

Skip unless time is left

Passive of causatives (have/get sth done), cognate-object verbs, passive of need/want + gerund ("The car needs washing"), and "it is said that…" vs "He is said to…" with perfect infinitives ("He is said to have been…").
