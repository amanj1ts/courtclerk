1. Snapshot

Weight: about 1.1 idiom questions per shift. Of 43 questions in the file, about 30 are idiom or phrase items. Formats seen: (a) replace the underlined part with an idiom, (b) meaning of a given idiom, (c) the idiom is in the options and the sentence gives the clue.

  1. Read the sentence and paraphrase the underlined part in plain words first.
  2. Read all four options before choosing; the traps are real idioms with the wrong meaning.
  3. Use the mood: angry, secret, risky, friendly, work, ill, happy, failing.
  4. Drop options whose literal picture clashes (e.g. "raining heavily" for under the weather).
  5. Check tone: positive vs negative. On top of the world fits a win, under the weather does not.
  6. For replace-type, the idiom must fit grammatically (tense, verb form).
  7. If two seem right, pick the one matching the most specific word in the sentence (e.g. "gracefully" → humble pie).
  8. "Secretly" points to in cahoots; "openly make peace" points to bury the hatchet.
  9. Never leave it on literal meaning; idioms are figurative.
  10. Negative marking is 1/4: guess only after removing two options.

5. Traps and memory aids

  • Gauntlet: throw down = issue; take up = accept. S18 offered "accept a challenge" as the trap.
  • Under the weather vs on top of the world: S14 put the first in a sentence about winning an award; opposites.
  • Bury the hatchet (peace, open) vs in cahoots (secret teaming) vs turn a blind eye (ignore).
  • Bite the bullet (endure pain) vs take the bull by the horns (tackle boldly) vs bite off more than you can chew (overload).
  • Ate humble pie (admit defeat meekly) vs threw in the towel (give up).
  • Let the cat out of the bag (accidental leak) vs spill the beans (similar) vs hook, line and sinker (gullible).
  • Mnemonic: PLAY with FIRE = Peril; NIP in the BUD = stop Now; BURN midnight OIL = Overtime.
  • Examiners paraphrase the meaning in the options with soft words ("slightly", "gradually", "especially"); the extreme-sounding correct one is often right (S12 knock the bottom out = severely damage).
