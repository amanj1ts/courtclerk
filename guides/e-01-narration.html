Weight: ~1.9 Qs per shift (42 Qs across 22 shifts) 🔥. Style: fill-in-the-blank with a two-clause direct sentence, or “identify the correct indirect form”. Options differ in tense, pronoun, reporting verb or word order.

  1. Find the sentence type: statement, question, command/request, exclamation, wish, suggestion.
  2. Pick the reporting verb for that type (said/told, asked, ordered/requested/advised, exclaimed, suggested, prayed, wished).
  3. Pick the link: that / if–whether / wh-word / to-infinitive / gerund.
  4. Change pronouns (SON: Subject-Object-No change).
  5. Backshift tense only if the reporting verb is past.
  6. Change time and place words.
  7. Question → statement word order, no “?”.
  8. Check exceptions: universal truth, past perfect, would/could/should/might, used to.
  9. Eliminate options with leftover “?” order, wrong verb or unchanged “tomorrow/today”.
  10. Do not drop the reporting verb meaning (e.g., “Let us” = suggested, not ordered).

3. Rules and concept notes

📘 Rule box (the WHY)

Indirect speech retells words from another time and person’s viewpoint. If the reporting verb is past, the speech moves one step back in time (backshift). If it is present/future (He says…), tense does not change. Pronouns follow viewpoint (SON), time/place words follow the new “now” and “here”.

  • Statement: said / said to → told + that.
  • Yes/No question: asked + if/whether + subject + verb. Wh-question: asked + same wh-word + subject + verb. No “?”, no do/does/did unless the verb needs it (did → had + V3).
  • Command: ordered/told/instructed + object + to V1; negative: … + not to V1 (no “don’t”). Request: requested/begged/urged. Advice: advised. Warning: warned. Prohibition: forbade + object + to V1 (no “not”).
  • Exclamation: exclaimed/cried/said with joy / sorrow / surprise / admiration / disgust that + statement. Drop Alas!, Hurrah!, How, What a (add “very” or “a great”).
  • “Let us / Let’s”: suggested/proposed + V-ing (or that we/they should + V1). “Let him/them”: ordered/said that he/they should.
  • Optative (wish/prayer): prayed/wished that + may → might. Greeting: wished/greeted/thanked/congratulated/welcomed + object. “Shall I…?” : offered to / asked if he should.

⚠️ Exceptions box (no backshift)

  • Universal truth: He said, “The sun rises in the east.” → that the sun rises in the east. Also scientific facts, proverbs.
  • Habitual action / still-true fact may stay present.
  • Past perfect stays past perfect (had been → had been).
  • would, could, should, might, ought to stay unchanged. “used to” stays.
  • must: obligation → had to (🔥 tested). Deduction (“She must be at home”) → stays must.
  • Reporting verb present/future (says, will say) → no tense change.
  • Unreal conditionals (2nd/3rd) and “I wish…” past forms stay.
  • If the reported time is still current, “today/now” may stay (rare in exams).

Mixed sentences and quotes within speech ⭐

Two clauses: change each by its own type, repeat the link where the types differ. He said, “I am tired and I will go to bed.” → He said that he was tired and that he would go to bed. Quotation inside speech: backshift both layers, She said, “He told me, ‘I am busy.’” → She said that he had told her that he was busy. Conditional in speech: “If I get time, I shall call you.” → said that if he got time, he would call me. Question + command mix: use asked … and told/requested … separately.

5. Memory aids

  • SON: Subject, Object, No change (pronouns).
  • “Past reports, past moves back”: present→past→past perfect.
  • Exclamation = “Drop and Declare”: drop Alas/What/How, declare with that.
  • Let us → Suggest -ing; Don’t → Warn not to; Please → Request to; Must → Had to.
  • Confusable: said to → told; forbade (no “not”) vs warned … not to; advised (infinitive) vs suggested (gerund: “advised me reducing” ✗).
  • Confusable: if vs whether: both fine for yes/no; “whether … or not” prefers whether.
  • “tomorrow” → the next day, “yesterday” → the previous day.

6. Trap alerts

  • 🔥 Wrong reporting verb: “Let us” with ordered/told; “Do not” with said; question with told.
  • 🔥 Leftover question order: “asked why was he late”, “if had I completed”.
  • 🔥 Unchanged time words: “today”, “tomorrow”, “here” left in the option.
  • 🔥 “suggested to do” / “suggested that let us”; “to not enter”; “said them”.
  • ⭐ Backshifting past perfect or a universal truth; changing “must” of deduction.
  • ⭐ Keeping “?”; adding “that” before a wh-word; repeating “not” after forbade.
  • ⭐ Pronoun traps: “my” must become “his/her” after a third-person subject.

🚫 10 common error patterns (before the practice set)

  1. He asked me that where I was going.
  2. She asked why was he late → why he was late.
  3. He told to me that… → told me that.
  4. He said me → said to me / told me.
  5. advised me reducing → to reduce.
  6. suggested to go → going.
  7. warned them to not → not to.
  8. forbade us not to park → forbade us to park.
  9. asked if where → drop if with wh-words.
  10. “tomorrow” kept after said (past) → the next day.

8. 60-second revision card

  1. Past reporting verb → backshift; present/future → none.
  2. am/is/are→was/were; have/has→had; will→would; can→could; may→might.
  3. did / past → had + V3; past perfect stays.
  4. must (duty) → had to; must (guess) stays.
  5. Universal truth / habit: no change.
  6. would, could, should, might, ought to, used to: unchanged.
  7. Pronouns: SON.
  8. now→then; today→that day; tomorrow→the next day; yesterday→the previous day.
  9. here→there; this→that; ago→before.
  10. Yes/No → asked + if/whether + S + V.
  11. Wh → asked + same wh-word + S + V; no “?”.
  12. Command → ordered/told + obj + to V1.
  13. Negative command → warned/ordered … not to V1.
  14. Request → requested; advice → advised.
  15. Let us → suggested/proposed + V-ing; Let us not → suggested not + V-ing.
  16. Exclamation → exclaimed with joy/sorrow/admiration/surprise that…
  17. Optative → prayed/wished that … might.
  18. Greeting → wished/greeted/thanked + object.
  19. said to → told (with object).
  20. Never write “that” before wh-words or “?” in indirect speech.
