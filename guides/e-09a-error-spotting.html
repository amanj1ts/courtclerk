1. Snapshot

About 2–3 questions per shift: tense/aspect choice, conditionals, “no sooner”, subject–verb agreement, adjective order, articles/prepositions in rectification. Many are “replace the underlined part” with 4 options.

  1. Find the clue word first: by the time, by next year, yesterday, if, unless, neither…nor, each.
  2. Find the real subject (ignore “of…”, “along with…”).
  3. Stative verb? Never continuous.
  4. “By + future point” → future perfect. “This time tomorrow” → future continuous.
  5. After if/when/by the time/as soon as: present for future.
  6. Third conditional: had + V3 in the if-clause, never “would have”.
  7. “No sooner” / “hardly” starts → inversion with had.
  8. Check adjective order, then adverb vs adjective.
  9. Check pronoun, preposition and spelling last.
  10. Eliminate options that break a rule; pick the one left, and skip if unsure (¼ negative).

3–5. Rules, wrong → right, with the examiner’s trap

Rule box

Core rules: stative verbs take simple tenses · time clauses use present for future · by + future time = future perfect · if-clause never takes will/would · each/every/one of + singular verb · neither…nor / either…or: verb agrees with the nearer subject · verbs need adverbs, nouns need adjectives · adjectives follow Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose.

Exceptions box

  • “A number of” = plural verb; “the number of” = singular.
  • Stative verbs can turn dynamic: “She is thinking/having lunch/seeing a doctor” is fine.
  • News, physics, mathematics, measles look plural but take singular verbs; scissors, trousers, spectacles are plural.
  • “Each” + “their” is common in speech; the shift key still chose “his or her”.
  • “Unless” is already negative: no second “not”.
  • Mixed conditional: past if-clause, present result: “If he had studied, he would be a doctor now.”
  • Subjunctive: “If I were…”, “I wish I were…”.
