  8. Governor cannot pardon court-martial or death sentence; President can.
  9. SC judges retire at 65, HC at 62.

5. Memory aids

  • 233-234-235-236: 233 Appoint (Governor+HC), 234 Others (PSC+HC), 235 Control (HC), 236 Define.
  • Art 32 vs 226: 32 is a Fundamental Right itself (SC must hear); 226 is discretionary (HC may refuse), wider scope.
  • Jurisdiction: Original = first hearing (131 Centre vs State). Appellate = review of lower court (132-134, 136 SLP). Advisory = President asks (143), non-binding.
  • Pardon five: Pardon, Commutation, Remission, Respite, Reprieve. President (72) all; Governor (161) all except court-martial and death-sentence pardon.
  • Retirement: SC 65 (S=Sixty-five), HC 62. Also CAT chairman 65, members 62.
  • Confusable: Art 129 (SC court of record) vs 215 (HC); Art 131 (original) vs 32 (writs); NALSA vs SC Legal Services Committee.

6. Trap alerts

  • “Which body supervises Lok Adalats nationally?” → NALSA, not Ministry of Law or SC committee (only 40% got it).
  • Art 161 is asked as “beyond Governor’s authority” → court-martial (Q4) / death-sentence pardon.
  • Pardon power is “judicial power” of the Governor, not executive or emergency (Q9, 23%).
  • Art 233 and 235 are swapped as options; read “appoint” vs “control”.
  • 323A vs 323B swapped; 42nd Amendment gave both.
  • Lok Adalat is NOT for punishing; it settles by compromise.
  • Original jurisdiction question where Art 32/226/143 are distractors for Art 131.
  • “Judicial activism” vs “judicial review” vs “overreach”: review = checks constitutionality; activism = proactive interpretation; overreach = encroachment.
