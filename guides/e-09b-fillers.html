1. Snapshot

Weight: ~2.9 Qs/shift (largest English block). Difficulty seen: ~35% easy / 34% moderate / 31% hard. CBT, 1 mark, −¼.

Legend: 🔥 seen in shifts / must-cover · ⭐ neighbour of a 🔥 concept · ◻ possible, not yet seen. Use the filter above.

How the exam asks it: (1) article pairs/triples – most frequent; (2) single word in context (adverb/adjective); (3) conjunction/connector; (4) preposition collocation; (5) tense/modal/agreement inside a blank.

10-line approach

  1. Read the whole sentence; ignore options first.
  2. Decide the part of speech: before noun = adjective/article; before verb/adjective = adverb; between clauses = conjunction; before noun phrase after verb/adj = preposition.
  3. Articles: look at the SOUND of the next word (honest, hour = an; university, European, one = a).
  4. Noun followed by "of …" or already specific, unique, landmark, range, superlative → the.
  5. Contrast signal (but, despite, although, yet, whereas) → pick opposite-meaning word. Cause signal (because, so) → consistent word.
  6. Time clue "by next week" → future perfect; "for hours before" → past perfect continuous.
  7. Subject-verb: plural subject → have; modal + base verb; modal + be + V3 for passive.
  8. Eliminate: wrong part of speech, wrong tone (reckless vs careful), wrong collocation.
  9. Pair blanks: test each blank separately, eliminate options by the easier blank.
  10. If two options fit, choose the more common collocation. Guess only after removing 2 (−¼ rule).

3. Concept notes: which part of speech fits?

🔥 Articles

  • an before vowel SOUND: an honest, an hour, an M.Phil., an ML-based, an unbiased, an ordinary, an aim.
  • a before consonant sound (even vowel letters): a university, a European, a one-day, a humble, a new.
  • the: mountain ranges (the Himalayas), landmarks (the Eiffel Tower), rivers/oceans, historical periods (the Renaissance), superlatives, "the + noun + of…" (the nature of, the search for), unique things (the world, the sun), qualified meals (the wedding breakfast).
  • No article: continents/most countries (Europe), abstract plurals, possessive + noun (their limitations), plain meals.

🔥 Conjunctions & connectors

Ask the relationship: contrast (but, yet, although, whereas, however) · reason (because) · result (therefore, so) · condition (unless = if not, provided that) · time limit (until, when). Coordinating (but, yet, so, and) joins equal clauses; subordinating (although, because, unless) starts a dependent clause; however/therefore are adverbs and need ; or . before them.

🔥 Modals (+ nearby grammar seen in blanks)

  • must = strong obligation/deduction; should / ought to = advice (ought + to + V1); need (modal in negatives/questions: need not go); dare (How dare you…; dare not); would = past habit/polite/conditional; could = past ability/possibility.
  • Modal + base verb always: must submit, must revise. Passive: modal + be + V3 (can be challenged).
  • Plural subject (The investigators/minutes) → have identified/have remained.
  • Whoever = subject of its own clause; whom = object; Which = choice between two.

🔥 Words in context

Underline clue words, predict the meaning, then match. Check collocation and tone. Adverb blank: modifies verb (-ly). Frequency adverbs (seldom, usually) sit before the main verb. "Despite/Although" → contrast; "so…that" → result consistent with the adjective.

5. Memory aids

  • Sound rule: "an hour, an honest, a unit, a user, a one-off" – say it aloud.
  • Correlative pairs: either–or, neither–nor, not only–but also, whether–or, scarcely/hardly–when, no sooner–than, both–and.
  • Confusables: beside (next to) / besides (also); among (3+) / between (2); amid (surrounded by chaos); beneath = under; since (point) / for (period); due to (noun) / owing to.
  • unless = if … not (never add "not" after unless).
  • Sequence connectors: first → then/next → subsequently → finally; meanwhile = at the same time; afterwards, thereafter, eventually (in the end).

6. Trap alerts

  • Vowel LETTER vs vowel SOUND (university, M.Phil., hour).
  • "blame on" vs "blame for"; "accused of" vs "charged with"; "different from" (not "than").
  • depends on/upon – never "over/for/towards".
  • "If … will" or "when … will" in time/condition clauses → wrong; use present.
  • Because vs therefore: both look causal; check which clause is the cause.
  • Near-synonym adjectives: pristine ≠ vivifying; exhilirated is misspelt.
  • Neither…nor: verb agrees with the nearer subject.
  • Unless vs although; although vs but: "although" and "but" are not used together.

  9. unless = if not · although/whereas = contrast
  10. therefore = result · until = time limit · but/yet = contrast
  11. Scarcely…when · No sooner…than · Not only…but also
  12. Neither…nor → nearer subject
  13. must + V1 · ought to + V1 · can be + V3
  14. whoever = subject, whom = object, which = choice
  15. by next week → will have + V3
  16. for hours before → had been + V-ing

  18. Adverb modifies verb: defensively, diligently
