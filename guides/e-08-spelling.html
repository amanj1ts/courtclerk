1. Snapshot

Almost every question gives one sentence with one misspelt word (often rare, Latin-rooted) and asks you to find it or pick its correct form. Four formats appeared: find the wrongly spelt word, pick the corrected spelling, pick the error from options, identify the misspelt pair (S7). Words were mostly advanced (inchoate, indefatigable, pejorative), with a few plain ones (referee, perseverance).

  1. Read the sentence once, ignore its meaning, and test each word letter by letter.
  2. Spot the word that "looks odd": usually a vowel swapped (a/e, i/e) or a letter missing.
  3. Check the ending first: -ance/-ence, -able/-ible, -ant/-ent, -ious/-uous.
  4. Check double letters: referee (one f), obsession (ss once), inaccuracies (cc, one r).
  5. Check silent letters (acquaintance, knowledge, exculpate).
  6. In "pick the correction" questions, the right option is the correct spelling, not the one in the sentence.
  7. Options often add new errors to words that were already right. Do not be fooled.
  8. Use word-families: obsess → obsession, correct → correlation, consequence → inconsequential.
  9. Say the word in syllables: per-se-ver-ance, ac-quaint-ance, mon-i-tor-ing.
  10. If unsure, leave it only if two options still look right: 1/4 negative marking.

3. Concept notes

  • Why root words help. Cohere → coherence; obsess → obsession; correct → correlation (co + relation, so co-rr-e-l); monitor → monitoring; substance → substantial.
  • -ance vs -ence. Verbs ending -ate or -ere give -ence (coherence, existence, correspondence). Words after -ar, -ur or hard c/g usually take -ance (perseverance, acquaintance, resistance). Exceptions must be learned.
  • Double letters. Doubling happens after a short stressed vowel when adding an ending (occur → occurrence). Referee has no doubling: refer + ee.
  • -able vs -ible. -able is added to complete words (adorable, readable); -ible follows word-pieces (visible, responsible, indefatigable is the Latin -able exception).
  • Latin borrowings. Pejorative (pejor = worse), inchoate (from Latin inchoare), exculpate (ex + culpa = blame), ubiquitous (ubique = everywhere).

5. Memory aids

  • Confusable pairs: calendar (planner) vs calender (a pressing machine, rare); stationery (e = envelope, paper) vs stationary (a = stands, still); principal vs principle; complement vs compliment; affect vs effect.
  • Mnemonics: "Never believe a lie" (ie in believe); "C before E": receive; necessary = one Collar, two Sleeves (1 c, 2 s); accommodate = two Cots, two Mattresses; embarrass = two Red, two Smiles.
  • Sound traps: liaison has two i; ubiquitous has one i before -tous; tenuous = ten + uous; indefatigable ends -able.

6. Trap alerts

  • The option list may show the correct word while the sentence holds the wrong one (S2, S3, S12, S14). Choose the corrected form.
  • Other options insert fresh errors into correct words (transend, degital, esteam, winsed).
  • "Select the INCORRECTLY spelt word" vs "correctly spelt": read the stem twice.
  • Whole-sentence options (S21): check every word in all four sentences; the error can be a common word (calender).
  • Two-error questions (S7): the answer must name both misspelt words.
  • Look-alike endings: -ance/-ence, -ant/-ent, -ible/-able.
