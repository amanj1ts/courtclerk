The method

Study methods that fit this paper

Not generic advice. Each method maps to how this CBT is actually built: 100 marks in 120 minutes, four options, negative marking, and a qualifying English section.

01

Retrieve before you re-read

Recognition feels like knowledge. The exam asks you to produce an answer, under time, with no page in front of you.

DO THIS: read a section once, close the guide, write the 60-second card from memory, then check. Use “Show answer” only after committing.
02

Start from real shift data

Every guide opens with “Seen in the shifts” — concepts that actually appeared, with the exact trap the examiner used.

DO THIS: cover the answer column, say each answer aloud, and mark every miss. Repeat until the table is boring.
03

Space it: 1 → 3 → 7 → 21

One strong pass fades by exam day. Four short touches cost less than one re-read and stick far longer.

DO THIS: 15-minute revisits on days 1, 3, 7 and 21 — only the 60-second card plus 10 MCQs.
04

Keep a trap ledger

This paper recycles distractor patterns: Bhangar vs Khadar, Article 72 vs 161, Konkan vs Malabar, Gupta vs post-Gupta.

DO THIS: one page per subject — “trap → correct rule → one example”. Read it before mocks, not every day.
05

Triage with 🔥 → ⭐ → ◻

About 35% of questions are easy, 34% moderate, 31% hard. Time spent on obscure ◻ facts is time stolen from 🔥 marks.

DO THIS: make every 🔥 automatic, then ⭐. Treat ◻ as final-week filler. Never drop a high-weight subject for current affairs.
06

Practise at exam speed

100 marks in 120 minutes is a pacing test as much as a knowledge test. Untimed practice hides your real weakness.

DO THIS: 50 GK in 45 min, 20 English in 20 min, 30-mark subjective in 45 min, 10 min buffer. No pauses.
07

Guess with maths, not fear

A ¼ penalty does not make guessing irrational. With four options, a blind guess still has positive expected value.

DO THIS: eliminate at least one option, then answer. Never leave a blank — see the maths below.
08

Protect the qualifiers daily

33% in each subject, 40% aggregate, plus a pass/fail CPT. A strong GK score cannot rescue a failed English section.

DO THIS: 15 min English drills, 15 min typing at 30+ WPM, 10 min spreadsheet. Non-negotiable, even on revision days.

Negative marking: the only maths you need

Expected marks per question, with 4 options and a ¼ penalty.

Blind guess+0.06
Eliminate 1 option+0.17
Eliminate 2 options+0.38
Leave it blank0
Rule: never leave a blank. Use the last seconds to eliminate one option — that roughly triples the return. Reserve “skip” for questions that would cost you three certain marks elsewhere.
The plan

Six-week run plan

Front-load the high-weight, high-predictability subjects. Keep revision in the last week — no new topics after day 35.

Week 1

Polity core

GS-01a + GS-01b. Output: 60 MCQs, one trap page (FR vs DPSP, Article 32 vs 226, Article 72 vs 161).

Week 2

Systems & history

GS-01c + GS-04. Output: 60 MCQs, one timeline sheet from 1857 to 1947 with Acts and viceroys.

Week 3

Geography

GS-05a. Output: blank-map labels for ranges, rivers and coasts; 40 MCQs; one page of superlatives.

Week 4

Economy & science

Add the economy and science modules as they land. Output: 50 MCQs, one formula-and-fact sheet.

Week 5

Current affairs + English subjective

Two essays, five letters, five précis and ten Punjabi-to-English passages — all typed, all timed.

Week 6

Revision only

Three full mocks, the 60-second cards, and the trap ledger. No new material, no new sources.

Non-negotiables

Every week, whatever the topic.

  • 15 min English drill, daily
  • 15 min typing + 10 min spreadsheet, daily
  • Three trap-ledger entries per study day
  • One timed mock every Sunday, reviewed for 90 min
  • No new source after day 35
If a week slips: cut new topics — never cut the mock or the daily English.
Reality check: the five guides available today cover Weeks 1–3. Weeks 4–6 assume the remaining modules are generated from the same prompt pack. If they are not ready, replace them with full mocks and revision — do not fill the gap with random YouTube playlists.
The library

Five guides, one structure

Every guide follows the same nine-part layout: snapshot → seen in the shifts → concept notes → fact tables → memory aids → trap alerts → practice set → 60-second card → verify box.

Order matters: start with GS-01b. For a court clerk post, judiciary is the highest-priority topic and the deepest question pool. GS-01a and GS-01c complete the polity block; GS-04 is the easiest GS topic by candidate accuracy; GS-05a is the highest-yield geography file.
Test day

Exam-day protocol

Budget the 120 minutes.45 min GK · 20 min English objective · 45 min subjective · 10 min buffer. Write these four numbers on the rough sheet first.
Use three passes.Pass 1: answer certainties. Pass 2: answer after elimination. Pass 3: flagged questions only. Never let one hard question eat four minutes.
Never leave a blank.Apply the maths: a blind guess is +0.06, one elimination is +0.17, two eliminations is +0.38. Blank is zero.
Protect both qualifiers.33% in each subject and 40% aggregate. English subjective is evaluated only if GK clears 33%, so neither section can be sacrificed.
Type cleanly, then stop.Target 30 WPM in the CPT. In the subjective part, leave five minutes to fix spellings, sentence boundaries and the letter format — not to rewrite content.
